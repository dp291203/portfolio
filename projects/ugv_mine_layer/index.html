UGV (Indian Army Collaboration)

A semi-autonomous Unmanned Ground Vehicle developed in collaboration with the Indian Army. Details are confidential. Top 30 of 260+ innovations at the Northern Command Inno Yodha Competition.

Note: This project was carried out in collaboration with the Indian Army and its technical details are confidential. The summary below is intentionally kept high-level.

Overview

From May to July 2025, I worked with a 8-member multidisciplinary team to design, fabricate, and field-test a semi-autonomous Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV) in collaboration with an Engineer Regiment of the Indian Army. The goal was to automate a hazardous field engineering task so that it could be performed without putting personnel at risk.

The work spanned mechanical design, embedded control, and autonomous/semi-autonomous operation, integrated into a single rugged platform built to survive real field conditions.

My Contribution

Beyond leading the team, my hands-on work focused on the embedded controls and software/autonomy side of the platform:

  • Embedded control system — the low-level control governing motion and the platform’s actuated mechanisms, with safety interlocks and an operator override at all times.
  • Semi-autonomous operation — the software logic that lets the vehicle carry out its task sequence with minimal operator input, while keeping a human in the loop.
  • Sensing & navigation — integrating onboard sensing for navigation and situational awareness, and the control link between the operator station and the vehicle.

(Specific architectures, components, and operational details are withheld as the project is confidential.)

Field Testing

The UGV was field-tested across multiple operationally representative environments, including high-altitude sub-zero conditions in Ladakh (~4,500 m), as well as in Rajasthan and Punjab, validating its performance across very different terrains and climates.

Recognition

The project was presented at the Northern Command Inno Yodha Competition, where it was selected among the Top 30 out of 260+ submitted innovations.

What I Learned

Working in an 8-person team across mechanical, electronics, and software domains under a hard deadline and in demanding field conditions was the most cross-disciplinary engineering experience of my undergraduate years — and a lesson in systems integration, teamwork, and building hardware that has to work the first time, in the field.
